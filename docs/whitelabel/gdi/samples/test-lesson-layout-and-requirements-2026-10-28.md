I think the best way to approach this is to create a 20–30 minute
AI-powered English lesson that demonstrates a complete learning
experience.

I'd like to focus on demonstrating five things:

1.  Personalization: The AI introduces the lesson, conducts a one-time
    English level check, and adjusts the lesson to the student's
    ability. When the level is at a beginner level/lower intermediate
    level, the AI uses Japanese when needed to avoid any frustration,
    etc.

2.  Interactive learning: The AI introduces a topic, asks questions,
    responds naturally, and encourages the student to speak.

3.  Grammar in context: The AI teaches specific grammar points and helps
    the student use them in realistic situations.

4.  Immediate feedback: The AI corrects mistakes, explains why something
    is incorrect, and gives the student a chance to try again. Using
    Japanese explanations when needed.

5.  Pronunciation practice: The student can choose to practice
    pronunciation and receive feedback on their spoken English. This is
    important for the specific sounds that many Japanese speakers
    struggle with when using the English language.

The key selling point is that the AI acts as a personal English coach,
not just a conversation partner. It should guide the student when
necessary but allow the conversation to develop naturally.

I'd also make the initial level check happen only once. After that, the
AI should maintain the student's assigned level throughout the lesson,
adjusting the complexity of its language, questions, and corrections
without repeatedly testing them.

# Proposed lesson: English for Professional Communication

# **AI English Training Demo**

## Lesson: Talking About Your Work and Recent Projects

Duration: 25 minutes (adaptable to 20–30 minutes)\
Format: One-to-one AI English lesson\
Target audience: Adult Japanese professionals learning English\
Lesson style: Adaptive, conversational, and student-centered\
Primary objective: Demonstrate how an AI English tutor can assess a
student's level, personalize instruction, teach grammar, facilitate
conversation, provide corrections, and offer pronunciation practice.

# 

# **PART 1 — LESSON OVERVIEW**

Time: 0:00–2:00 \| Approximately 2 minutes

### AI Tutor: Welcome and Introduction

The AI should begin by welcoming the student and presenting a short
overview of the lesson before asking any assessment questions.

Example AI introduction:

"Hello, and welcome to your English lesson!

Today, we're going to practice talking about your work and the projects
you're involved in.

In this lesson, we'll practice explaining what you do, describing your
work experience, and talking about projects you've worked on.

We'll also practice asking questions and keeping a conversation going.

Here is what we'll cover today:

1\. Speaking practice\
You'll answer questions about your job, responsibilities, and work
experiences.

2\. Grammar\
We'll practice two useful grammar points:

- The present perfect: talking about experiences and achievements.

- The past simple: talking about completed activities and specific
  events in the past.

3\. Conversation skills\
You'll practice asking and answering follow-up questions to make
conversations more natural.

4\. Personalized feedback\
I'll help you improve your grammar, vocabulary, and sentence structure.
I'll also explain your mistakes and give you opportunities to try again.

5\. Pronunciation practice\
If you'd like, you can practice pronouncing words and sentences and
receive feedback on your pronunciation.

Before we begin, I'll check your English level so I can adjust the
lesson to suit you. This is a one-time check for today's lesson. After
that, we'll move directly into the activities.

By the end of this lesson, you should be better able to talk about your
work and professional experiences in English.

Ready? Let's get started!"

### Design requirements

The AI should:

- Clearly explain the lesson's topics and learning objectives.

- Identify the specific grammar points before teaching them.

- Explain how feedback and optional pronunciation practice work.

- Use simple, natural language rather than a long, formal explanation.

- Avoid starting the level assessment until the overview is complete.

# **PART 2 — ONE-TIME ENGLISH LEVEL CHECK**

Time: 2:00–5:00 \| Approximately 3 minutes

### Objective

Determine the student's approximate English proficiency and use the
results to personalize the remainder of the lesson.

The AI should assess the student through a short, natural conversation
rather than a traditional multiple-choice test.

### **Step 1:** Begin the assessment

AI:

"First, I'd like to learn a little about your English level.

Please answer my questions in English. Don't worry about making
mistakes. I want to understand what you can already do so I can make
this lesson useful for you.

Let's start with an easy question.

Question 1: Can you tell me a little about your job? What do you do?"

Student example — Beginner:

"I work in sales. I sell machines."

AI behavior:

- Recognize that the student can communicate basic information.

- Avoid overwhelming the student with complex follow-up questions.

- Ask a simple follow-up question.

AI:

"Thank you! What kind of machines do you sell?"

Student example — Intermediate:

"I work in sales for a company that manufactures packaging machines. I
mainly communicate with customers and help them find the right
products."

AI behavior:

- Recognize the student's ability to produce connected sentences and
  explain responsibilities.

- Ask a more challenging follow-up question.

AI:

"That sounds interesting. Can you tell me about a recent project you
worked on and explain what your responsibilities were?"

### **Step 2:** Test conversational ability

AI:

"Here's another question.

Question 2: Have you ever worked with an international customer? If so,
can you tell me about the experience?"

If the student has no experience with international customers, the AI
should accept an alternative example, such as communicating with a new
customer or working on a difficult project.

The AI should observe:

- Whether the student understands the question.

- Whether the student can answer without extensive assistance.

- Grammar accuracy.

- Vocabulary range.

- Sentence complexity.

- Ability to explain an experience.

- Ability to maintain a conversation.

**Step 3:** Final assessment question

AI:

"Great! I have one final question.

Question 3: What is one skill you'd like to improve in your English, and
why is it important to you?"

This question assesses the student's ability to explain an opinion and
provide a reason.

### **Step 4:** Assign a working level

After the three questions, the AI should assign an approximate working
level based on the student's responses.

For a demonstration, use three broad levels:

| **Working level** | **Typical characteristics** | **How the AI should adapt** |
|----|----|----|
| Beginner | Uses short sentences and familiar vocabulary. May need help understanding questions or forming answers. | Ask simple questions, introduce useful phrases, explain grammar in plain English, and allow more thinking time. |
| Intermediate | Can communicate in connected sentences but makes grammatical or vocabulary errors. | Use follow-up questions, encourage longer answers, and explain common errors. |
| Advanced | Communicates fluently with varied vocabulary and complex sentences. | Ask more challenging questions, explore nuances, and focus on precision, natural phrasing, and professional communication. |

These are practical working categories, not formal CEFR certification.
The AI should use the initial responses to estimate the student's
proficiency and adjust its behavior accordingly.

Example AI response:

"Thank you! Based on your answers, I'd estimate your current English
level as intermediate.

You can explain your work and communicate your ideas, but we'll focus on
making your grammar more accurate and your answers more natural.

I'll adjust the questions and explanations to suit your level throughout
the lesson.

Let's move on to our first activity!

### Important AI behavior

The AI should:

- Perform this level check only once per lesson.

- Avoid announcing a definitive proficiency level based on three short
  answers.

- Adjust its vocabulary, speaking prompts, explanations, and correction
  style to the student's ability.

- Continue to adapt to the student's performance without restarting the
  assessment.

- Avoid giving lengthy corrections during the assessment itself, so the
  student can demonstrate their natural ability.

# **PART 3 — TOPIC INTRODUCTION**

Time: 5:00–8:00 \| Approximately 3 minutes

### Topic: Talking About Your Work and Experience

The AI now introduces the subject and begins teaching through
conversation.

The explanation should change according to the student's assigned level.

### Beginner version

AI:

"Let's learn how to talk about your work.

We can use simple sentences to explain our jobs.

For example:

- I work in sales.

- I help customers.

- I work with international clients.

- I joined my company three years ago.

Let's practice.

Question 1: What do you do at work?

Try to answer using one or two sentences."

After the student responds, the AI should acknowledge the answer,
correct any important mistakes, and ask another question.

### Intermediate version

AI:

"When we talk about our work, we often explain our responsibilities,
experience, and achievements.

For example:

'I work in sales, and I help customers find the right products.'

We can also talk about our experience:

'I have worked with international customers for three years.'

Notice the difference between these two sentences:

- 'I have worked with international customers for three years.'

- 'I worked with an international customer yesterday.'

The first sentence describes experience continuing over a period of
time. The second describes a completed activity at a specific time in
the past.

We'll practice this difference during today's lesson.

Question 1: What are your main responsibilities at work?

Try to give me a complete answer with at least two sentences."

### Advanced version

AI:

"When discussing our professional experience, we need to choose the
right tense to communicate the timing and relevance of an event.

For example:

'I have worked with international customers for several years.'

This emphasizes experience accumulated over time.

Compare that with:

'I worked with a major international customer last year.'

This describes a completed experience in a specific past period.

Today, we'll focus on using these tenses accurately while discussing
professional responsibilities and achievements.

Question 1: Could you describe your current responsibilities and explain
how your role has developed over time?

### Design requirements

The AI should select one version based on the initial assessment rather
than presenting all three to the student.

The examples should introduce the grammar naturally. The AI should then
move directly into conversation, using the student's responses to
determine what to explain next.

# **PART 4 — CONVERSATIONAL PRACTICE AND GRAMMAR**

Time: 8:00–15:00 \| Approximately 7 minutes

### Objective

Help the student use the target grammar in a realistic conversation
rather than completing isolated grammar exercises.

The AI should ask one question at a time, respond to the student's
answers, correct important mistakes, and encourage the student to
elaborate.

## Grammar Point 1: Present Perfect

Form:

Subject + have/has + past participle

Examples:

- I have worked here for five years.

- She has completed several projects.

- We have worked with international customers.

Main use for this lesson:

Talking about experience, achievements, and activities that began in the
past and continue into the present.

### Example conversation

AI:

"Let's talk about your work experience.

Have you worked for your current company for a long time?"

Student:

"Yes, I work here for five years."

AI:

"Good! I understand what you mean.

A more natural way to say this is:

'I have worked here for five years.'

We use 'have worked' because you started working here in the past and
you still work here now.

Let's try again.

Can you say the complete sentence using 'I have worked...'?

Student:

"I have worked here for five years."

AI:

"Excellent! Now let's continue.

What is one important skill you have learned at your company?"

Student:

"I have learned how to communicate with customers."

AI:

"That's a good sentence! You used the present perfect correctly.

Can you tell me a little more about that skill? How has it helped you at
work?"

The AI should continue the conversation naturally, asking follow-up
questions based on the student's answers.

## Grammar Point 2: Past Simple

Form:

Subject + past-tense verb

Examples:

- I joined the company in 2022.

- We attended an international exhibition last month.

- I met a new customer yesterday.

Main use for this lesson:

Talking about completed events and activities at a specific time in the
past.

### Example conversation

AI:

"Now let's talk about a specific event.

Did you attend any business events or exhibitions last year?"

Student:

"Yes, I have attended an exhibition last year."

AI:

"Good answer! There's just one small grammar point to correct.

Because you're talking about a specific time in the past — last year —
we use the past simple.

Instead of:

'I have attended an exhibition last year.'

Say:

'I attended an exhibition last year.'

Let's try another question.

When was the last time you met an important customer?"

Student:

"I met an important customer last month."

AI:

"Great! You used the past simple correctly.

What did you discuss with the customer?"

The AI should use the student's answers to develop the conversation,
rather than rigidly following a predetermined script.

## Conversational Follow-Up Skills

Throughout this section, the AI should encourage the student to provide
more information.

For example:

AI: "Have you ever worked with a customer from another country?"

Student: "Yes, I have."

AI: "Great! Can you tell me a little about that experience?"

Student: "I worked with a customer from Germany."

AI: "Interesting! When did you first meet the customer?"

Student: "I met him at an exhibition last year."

AI: "What did you discuss at the exhibition?"

This creates a natural conversation while reinforcing the difference
between discussing experience and describing specific past events.

### Adaptive difficulty

The AI should adjust the conversation based on the student's responses.

If the student struggles:

- Ask simpler questions.

- Offer sentence starters.

- Explain unfamiliar vocabulary.

- Break complicated questions into smaller parts.

Example:

"Try starting with: 'I have worked with...'"

If the student performs well:

- Ask more detailed follow-up questions.

- Encourage longer answers.

- Introduce more challenging vocabulary.

- Ask the student to explain reasons or compare experiences.

Example:

"What challenges have you encountered when working with international
customers, and how did you overcome them?"

The AI should not force every student to complete the same number of
questions. The priority is meaningful practice at an appropriate level.

# **PART 5 — OPEN CONVERSATION PRACTICE**

Time: 15:00–20:00 \| Approximately 5 minutes

### Objective

Give the student a less structured opportunity to use what they have
learned.

At this stage, the AI should behave more like a conversation partner and
less like a teacher delivering a lesson.

It should continue to support learning, but the student should have more
control over the conversation.

### AI introduction

AI:

"You're doing well! Now let's put everything together.

I'd like to have a more natural conversation with you about your work.

I'll ask you questions, and you can ask me questions, too. Try to use
the grammar we've practiced, but don't worry about being perfect.

I'll help you when you need it.

Let's start here:

What is one project you've worked on that you're particularly proud of?"

### Example conversation

Student:

"I worked on a project to improve our customer service."

AI:

"That sounds interesting! What was your role in the project?"

Student:

"I was responsible to communicate with our customers."

AI:

"I understand. A more natural way to say this is:

'I was responsible for communicating with our customers.'

After 'responsible for,' we use a noun or a verb ending in '-ing.'

Can you try that sentence again?"

Student:

"I was responsible for communicating with our customers."

AI:

"Excellent! What was the biggest challenge you faced during the
project?"

Student:

"The biggest challenge was that we didn't have enough time."

AI:

"That makes sense. How did you handle the situation?"

Student:

"We changed the schedule and finished the project on time."

AI:

"Well done! You explained the problem and the solution clearly.

Here's a question for you: Have you worked on a similar project since
then?"

The conversation continues naturally.

### Student-led interaction

The student should also be encouraged to ask the AI questions.

For example:

AI:

"Now it's your turn to ask me a question about work experience. You can
ask me anything related to today's topic."

Student:

"What kind of projects have you worked on?"

AI:

"That's a good question! I've helped with several types of projects,
including customer service improvements and team training.

What kind of project would you like to work on in the future?"

The AI can answer in a conversational style and then invite the student
to respond.

### Important design feature

The AI should not simply ask a question, receive an answer, and
immediately move to the next scripted question.

Instead, it should:

1.  Listen to the student's response.

2.  Identify useful information in the answer.

3.  Respond naturally to that information.

4.  Correct important errors when appropriate.

5.  Ask a relevant follow-up question.

6.  Allow the student to change the subject within the lesson's general
    theme.

This is an important opportunity to demonstrate the difference between a
conversational AI and an AI tutor designed specifically for language
learning.

# **PART 6 — OPTIONAL PRONUNCIATION PRACTICE**

Time: 20:00–23:00 \| Approximately 3 minutes

### Objective

Demonstrate how the student can switch from conversational practice to
pronunciation training without starting a new lesson.

Pronunciation should be an optional feature that the student can choose
when needed.

### Step 1: Offer the option

AI:

"Before we finish, would you like to practice your pronunciation?

We can work on words and sentences that are useful when talking about
your job.

You can choose:

1.  Practice individual words.

2.  Practice a complete sentence.

3.  Practice speaking more naturally.

4.  Skip pronunciation practice and continue our conversation.

What would you prefer?

The AI should wait for the student's choice.

### Option A: Individual word practice

The AI selects a useful word from the lesson, such as
"responsibilities."

AI:

"Let's practice the word 'responsibilities.'

Listen to the pronunciation, and then try saying the word yourself."

The student speaks.

AI feedback example:

"Good attempt! Let's work on the stress pattern. The main stress is on
the fourth syllable.

Try saying it again, paying attention to the stressed syllable."

The AI should then allow the student to repeat the word.

### Option B: Sentence practice

AI:

"Let's practice this sentence:

'I have worked with international customers for several years.'

First, listen to the sentence. Then repeat it in your own voice."

After the student speaks, the AI should provide feedback on aspects such
as:

- Individual sounds.

- Word stress.

- Sentence rhythm.

- Linking between words.

- Intelligibility.

Example feedback:

"Your sentence was understandable. Let's work on the phrase
'international customers.'

Try connecting the words more smoothly rather than separating every
word.

Let's try the complete sentence one more time."

The AI should focus on one or two specific improvements at a time rather
than giving the student a long list of pronunciation problems.

### Option C: Natural speech

The AI asks the student to repeat a complete answer from the
conversation.

AI:

"Now let's practice speaking naturally.

Please say this sentence:

'I was responsible for communicating with our customers and helping them
find the right solutions.'

Try to speak clearly and naturally, without rushing."

The AI provides targeted feedback based on the student's speech.

### Technical requirement

The system should distinguish between speech recognition and actual
pronunciation assessment.

If the AI can only transcribe the student's speech, it should not claim
to have measured individual sounds, stress, or intonation accurately.

Reliable pronunciation feedback requires suitable audio-processing
capabilities. The AI should only report features that its underlying
speech technology can assess.

# PART 7 — LESSON SUMMARY AND FEEDBACK

Time: 23:00–25:00 \| Approximately 2 minutes

### Objective

Finish the lesson with a short review of what the student learned and
personalized feedback.

The feedback should be based on the student's actual performance during
the lesson, not a generic list of compliments.

### Example AI conclusion

AI:

"Great work today! Let's quickly review what we practiced.

Today, you practiced talking about your work, responsibilities, and
professional experiences.

We focused on two grammar points:

1\. Present perfect

We use this to talk about experiences and activities connected to the
present.

For example: 'I have worked with international customers for three
years.'

2\. Past simple

We use this to describe completed activities at a specific time in the
past.

For example: 'I attended an exhibition last year.'

You also practiced explaining your responsibilities, answering follow-up
questions, and talking about a challenge you faced at work.

### Your feedback

One thing you did well was explaining your work experience clearly.

One area to improve is using the correct tense when talking about
specific times in the past.

For example, remember to say 'I attended an exhibition last year' rather
than 'I have attended an exhibition last year.'

### One final question

Before we finish, can you explain the difference between these two
sentences?

- 'I have worked here for five years.'

- 'I worked here five years ago.'

Take your time. I'll help you if you need it."

The AI should evaluate the student's answer and give a short explanation
if necessary.

AI closing:

"Well done! Thank you for participating in today's lesson.

Keep practicing these grammar points when talking about your work and
experiences.

See you next time!"

# **PART 8 — AI BEHAVIOR AND PERSONALIZATION RULES**

The following instructions define how the AI tutor should behave
throughout the demonstration.

These are intended as functional requirements for the AI training
platform rather than student-facing lesson content.

## 1. One-time level assessment

The AI must:

- Present the lesson overview first.

- Conduct a brief conversational assessment immediately afterward.

- Estimate the student's working proficiency level.

- Adapt the remainder of the lesson to that level.

- Avoid repeating the assessment during the same lesson.

## 2. Adaptive instruction

The AI should adjust:

- Vocabulary difficulty.

- Sentence length and complexity.

- Grammar explanations.

- Question difficulty.

- Amount of assistance provided.

- Expectations for student responses.

The student should not need to manually select a new level when the AI
has already assessed their ability.

## 3. Natural conversation

The AI should:

- Ask one question at a time.

- Listen to the student's complete response.

- Respond to the meaning of the answer.

- Ask relevant follow-up questions.

- Allow the student to ask questions.

- Avoid sounding like a rigid questionnaire.

## 4. Intelligent correction

When the student makes a mistake, the AI should generally follow this
pattern:

Acknowledge → Correct → Explain → Practice → Continue

For example:

Student: "I am working here since 2020."

AI:\
"I understand! A more natural way to say this is, 'I have worked here
since 2020.'

We use the present perfect with 'since' when describing an activity that
started in the past and continues now.

Can you try the sentence again?

Once the student demonstrates the correct form, the AI should continue
the conversation.

Not every minor error needs to interrupt the conversation. The AI should
prioritize mistakes that affect meaning, involve the target grammar, or
are particularly useful learning opportunities.

## 5. Personalized feedback

The AI should identify:

- What the student did well.

- Repeated grammar mistakes.

- Vocabulary that could be improved.

- Opportunities to express ideas more naturally.

- Areas for further practice.

Feedback should be specific to the student's responses.

## 6. Optional pronunciation mode

Pronunciation practice should:

- Be offered at an appropriate point in the lesson.

- Be available on request.

- Allow the student to practice individual words and complete sentences.

- Provide targeted feedback supported by the platform's audio
  capabilities.

- Allow the student to return to normal conversation afterward.

## 7. Student-centered pacing

The AI should adjust its pacing based on the student's responses.

If the student needs additional help, the AI can spend more time on
grammar and reduce the number of questions.

If the student performs well, the AI can increase the complexity of the
conversation.

The AI should prioritize learning outcomes over completing every planned
activity within a rigid schedule.

# FINAL DESIGN RECOMMENDATION

For the demonstration, I would prioritize three features above
everything else:

1\. Show that the AI understands the student's level.

The student should notice a meaningful difference between a beginner
experience and an intermediate or advanced experience. The initial
assessment should have a visible effect on the lesson.

2\. Show that the AI can teach, not just chat.

When the student makes a mistake, the AI should explain the problem,
help the student correct it, and then return to the conversation.

3\. Show that the AI can move naturally between learning modes.

The student should be able to move from a grammar explanation to
conversational practice, request pronunciation feedback, and return to
the conversation without losing the context of the lesson.

If the platform can demonstrate these three capabilities convincingly,
the example training will communicate the value of AI-powered English
training much more effectively than a standard conversation demo.
