The two findings that would have cost an outage, both in the rollout instructions rather than the code:

- A wrong-but-valid 32-byte `PORTAL_SECRET_ENC_KEY` seals cleanly, writes rows and exits 0; the server

**Step 7 was blocked for a measured reason — issue #9 — and both halves of that blocker are now
cleared.** What follows is the measurement that established the blocker; the resolution is below it.

Every `Partner` row was seeded with `{rpm 600, burst 60}`, and `burstCap = min(rpm, burst)`, so the
effective ceiling was 60 req/min per (partner, endpoint). Fourteen days of nginx logs show
`/api/portal/v1/session-progress` is bimodal: ~28-29 req/min for one poller (a request every ~2s),
**57-58 for two concurrent sessions — 96% of the cap** — and 0 minutes at or above 60. The traffic is
server-side (`"node"` UA, `?sessionId=portal-…`) and attributes to **crimsora**, the busiest partner.

So a third concurrent crimsora session is ~86 req/min on that one endpoint. Report-only serves it
today; enforcing turns it into `429`s on live session polling, and the failure only appears when three
sessions happen to overlap. Burst 60 with this polling pattern is an effective ceiling of two
concurrent sessions per partner — a product limit, not a config detail.

**Both of those are now resolved (2026-08-16).**

`apps/tutor/scripts/set-partner-limits.ts` (`npm run limits:set-partner`) is the edit path — dry-run by
default, `--write` applies, and it can write nothing but `limits` and `updatedAt`. It refuses to create
a row, refuses a partner whose row is missing, uses a compare-and-set filter so two concurrent runs
cannot silently last-write-wins, and fails loudly if the write matched zero documents. 76 tests, every
one mutation-proven.

Two defects were caught before it ran, both the same shape — the script disagreeing with `registry.ts`
about what a missing field means, and neither visible by reading:

- Its absent-`limits` fallback was `{rpm: 0, burst: 0}` against the registry's `{600, 60}`. Since
  `burst: 0` means *no burst limiting*, raising a cap on such a row would have **removed** it.
- The fix for that then over-corrected: on a row with a *partial* `limits`, `--rpm 1200` would have
  written `burst: 60` and **cut the live cap from 600 to 60** — with the command meant to raise it.
  Found by running against a real mongod, not by review.

It now derives the baseline the way the reader does: whole-subdocument absence takes
`ENV_FALLBACK_LIMITS`; a present-but-partial `limits` keeps its missing fields unset, and they
contribute no cap, exactly as `limits.ts` excludes them.

**crimsora is raised to `{rpm: 900, burst: 300}`** (effective cap 300/min, ~10 concurrent sessions at
the observed ~29/min each — about 5× the measured peak). Verified after the write: only `limits` and
`updatedAt` changed, `secrets`/`status`/`kind`/`allowedEndpoints`/`createdAt` intact, other rows
untouched. academy and evelyn-marketing remain at `{600, 60}`; they peak at 2 req/min, but they poll
the same endpoints and would meet the same ceiling if they ever get busy.

**What step 7 still wants** is not a blocker but a habit: a stretch of the weekly digest confirming
peaks stay well under the caps, and a decision on whether to raise academy and evelyn-marketing before
enforcing rather than after.

`PartnerCounter` is metering now, but its 48h TTL covers both window kinds, so history does not
accumulate on its own. **Two stopgap jobs run on production since 2026-08-16**, both in root's crontab
(prior crontab backed up at `/root/crontab.backup-2026-08-16`), logging to
`/var/log/partner-metering.log`:

| Cron (Europe/Berlin) | Job | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `7 2 * * *` | `/root/evelyn-tutor-ops/export-partner-metering.sh` | Appends a snapshot to `/root/partner-metering/partner-metering.jsonl` |
| `12 17 * * 1` | `/root/evelyn-tutor-ops/mail-partner-metering.js` | Emails a weekly digest |

**Both cron times encode a timezone offset that is load-bearing, not cosmetic.** `dayWindow()` in
`portal/limits.ts` keys the day document by the **UTC** date (`toISOString().slice(0, 10)`), while cron
on this box runs in Europe/Berlin (UTC+2). So the export runs at 02:07 local = **00:07 UTC**, just after
the UTC day closes; at 00:07 *local* it would snapshot the day two hours early and — since the next run
is 24h later — those two hours would never be captured at all. The digest runs 17:12 local = 08:12
America/Los_Angeles, i.e. Monday morning for the reader rather than late Sunday night.

The export writes two record kinds, because they answer different questions: `kind: "day"` (totals, for
quota and billing) and `kind: "minutePeak"` (the busiest single minute per partner/endpoint/day). **The
minute peak is the one step 7 turns on** — the cap is `min(rpm, burst)` per *minute*, so a daily total
says nothing about whether the cap would have been hit. Duplicate rows across runs are expected; take
the max `count` per `(kind, partnerId, endpoint, windowStart)`. Read it with:

```
ssh <prod-host> 'cat /root/partner-metering/partner-metering.jsonl' | \
  jq -s '[.[] | select(.kind == "minutePeak")] | group_by(.partnerId + .endpoint) | map(max_by(.count))'
```

The digest emails to `praveen@evelynlearning.com` (override with `METERING_DIGEST_TO`), reusing the deployed
app's own nodemailer and SMTP settings so nothing extra is installed. It explains what the reader is
looking at, flags any endpoint at or above 75% of its cap, and states that raising a limit currently
requires a hand-edit of the `partners` collection.

Both are stopgaps, not the export job #9 asks for: they live on the box rather than in the repo, have
no tests, and nothing alerts if they stop running.

**Do not flip `PORTAL_IDENTITY_RESOLUTION` back off.** Any student first seen while it was on lives
under a resolved id the raw-id path cannot find, so reverting orphans them, and a later
`backfill --write` can die on E11000 mid-loop.

## What the merge changed on deploy

Merging was safe, but it was **not** a no-op: three behaviours shipped the moment the code was
deployed, regardless of any flag.

- **The limiter runs from the first request.** `checkPartnerLimits` is called inside
  `withPortalAuth` for every portal call, and `PORTAL_LIMITS_MODE` **unset means enforce**. The
  deploy that shipped this branch carried `PORTAL_LIMITS_MODE=report-only` (spec §10 step 1); the flag
  semantics are deliberate and were not inverted, so the mitigation is procedural. The limiter logs one
  warning per process, the first time it runs with the flag unset, naming the effective caps — a
  forgotten flag is visible in the logs before it is visible as 429s.
- **Portal auth now reads Mongo** — a 60s-cached registry read plus two counter writes per request,
  where before it read `process.env` only. A registry read failure denies `401 unknown_partner` across
  every `/api/portal/v1/**` route (spec §3/§8), logged as `registry_unavailable` — or as
  `secret_key_unavailable` when the cause is a missing/unusable `PORTAL_SECRET_ENC_KEY`, which is a
  configuration fault and not a database one.
- **Four internal routes that had no auth at all before this branch now 401 a present-but-invalid
  embed token**: `topic-notes/[studentId]/[baselineId]`, its `[overlayId]` child,
  `student-profile/[id]/preferences`, and `mock-review-context`. Correct per spec §4.0, but it is a
  live behaviour change on an embed path.

## Rollout — read spec §10, it is the authority

Two corrections landed late and are easy to get wrong from memory:

- **`PORTAL_LIMITS_MODE=report-only` must be set in the SAME deploy that ships the branch.** Unset
  means *enforce*, and the limiter runs for every portal call the moment the code is live. Scheduling
  the observation window last means it observes nothing.
- **Removing `PORTAL_PARTNER_SECRETS` is NOT a rollout step and is blocked in M1c.** `getPartnerSecret`
  reads `process.env` only and never consults the registry; three call sites use it
  (`embed-token.ts`, `replay-token.ts`, the demo-token route) backing `verifyEmbedToken`, which gates
  the `tutor-portal/embed` page academy and Crimsora iframe plus six `/api/tutor/**` routes. Removing
  it returns `unknown partner` for every partner regardless of registry state.

Preconditions for the identity flip: `EMBED_TOKEN_ENFORCE=on` in the target environment (**production
already has it**), and the backfill + index build complete.

`PORTAL_SECRET_ENC_KEY` does not exist in any environment yet — rollout step 1 genuinely has not been
done. **Back it up with the other production secrets**; losing it means every partner re-keys.

## Follow-ups this milestone deliberately did not do

- **Secret rotation has no tooling** — tracked as **issue #7**. The seed's create-only field partition
  means nothing can add a second secret to an existing partner, and `PORTAL_SECRET_ENC_KEY` was
  generated for the first time on 2026-08-16, so the first rotation is imminent. Manual procedure in
  `README.md`; a command is M1e.
- **`defaultLimitsDeps.bump`'s E11000 retry is not hermetically tested** — it lives inside the real
  Mongo-backed `bump` that every test replaces by injection. Needs the raw upsert extracted into an
  injectable sub-part.
- **`verifyEmbedToken` applies none of the registry's `kind`/`status` checks** — tracked as
  **issue #8**. Now that identity resolution is live, an embed token picks a write namespace, so a
  suspended partner with a live env secret keeps writing into its own namespace after the portal API
  has cut it off. Closing it means migrating `getPartnerSecret`'s three call sites onto the registry,
  which is also the precondition for ever removing `PORTAL_PARTNER_SECRETS`.
- **Retail `/tutor` will 401 the profile routes the moment retail launches with persistent student
  ids.** Not live today — verified: **0** students have ever reached the tutor from a retail-only
  source; all 115 with a persistent id came via embed/partner. The route's own comment ("Add auth when
  retail launches") anticipates it.
- **The registry cache is module-level and keyed only by `partnerId`** — it is shared across
  injected `deps`, so a suite that swaps deps must call `invalidatePartner` first. Test-only coupling
  today; a real cache abstraction is post-M1c.
- **Secret expiry is evaluated at cache-fill time**, so a secret can keep authenticating for up to
  60s past its `expiresAt` (deliberate, documented in `registry.ts`). `invalidatePartner` is the
  immediate-revocation lever for a real compromise.
- `EvidenceEvent.partnerId` stays as-is (populated on 7 of 2,423). Nothing reads it.

## Things that cost time; do not rediscover them

- **Per-app env staging.** A fresh worktree needs `.env.local` in **each app directory**, not just the
  repo root — `apps/tutor/.env.local` and `apps/marketing/.env.local`, symlinks in `m1a-split`.
  Without it, `test:material-extract` fails on live vision calls and looks like a regression.
- **Mongoose `autoIndex`/`autoCreate` default on.** Merely connecting builds every declared index,
  including `TutorSession`'s TTL index that deletes rows. Both ops scripts now disable them first;
  any new script must too.
- **`tsconfig.json` excludes `scripts/`**, so `tsc -p` does not type-check them. `ts-node` does, at run
  time. Missing arguments in `scripts/` are invisible to `tsc`.
- **Stale per-worktree `node_modules`.** `r42-ship` had `@evelyn/portal-contract@1.13.0` against a
  manifest pinning 1.14.0, producing five phantom type errors. Check the installed version before
  believing a cross-worktree typecheck.
- **"Unprefixed id" ≠ "retail".** academy and crimsora send bare UUIDs, so classifying by id shape
  counts partner traffic as retail. Discriminate by `TutorSession.source` / `sourcePartnerId`.

## The pattern worth carrying forward

Every defect found in this milestone was in the plan's or spec's own text, not in the implementers'
work — twelve of them across seven tasks, including two auth holes and two rollout-order bugs that
would have caused outages. Three times a test shipped that **could not fail**, each caught by
mutation-testing rather than by reading. If you continue this work: keep the per-task review gate, and
when a reviewer says a test passes, ask whether it would fail.
