Exclusion shape: vertical by depth. Every theme below IS touched by this course, at analysis / evaluation / application depth on data or a case the item itself describes; what is withheld is the named model above, or the mechanism below (Grade 7 World Geography teaches every mechanism from zero and this course assumes it in one clause). An item whose key is a definition is below this course; an item that names one of the models below is above it.

- The stage-numbered demographic transition model — this course reads pyramids, computes a dependency ratio and evaluates projections; the model is AP Human Geography.
- Urban-structure models (concentric zone, sector, multiple nuclei) and gentrification — this course measures urban form and evaluates a growth proposal; the models are AP Human Geography.
- Agricultural land-use models and the formal agricultural-systems typology — this course compares two described farming systems on data; the models are AP Human Geography.
- State / nation / nation-state and supranational-organization analysis by name — this course analyzes a described dispute case and evaluates cooperation design; the typology is AP Human Geography.
- Industrial-location theory by name — this course decides a described factory siting on stated factors; the theory is high-school / AP.
- The greenhouse mechanism beyond define depth, and attribution — this course reads climate trend data (trend versus variability, uneven regional impact) and carries one define-depth paragraph in that row only; no radiative-forcing arithmetic, no attribution debate.
- Hazard physics and forecasting (how a hurricane, tornado or earthquake happens; weather-map forecasting) — this course maps hazard regions and evaluates risk as hazard × exposure × vulnerability; the physics is Grade 6 Science and Grade 7 plate mechanics.
- Water-cycle and groundwater mechanics — this course allocates a described basin's flow between users; the mechanism is Grade 6 Science.
- Population ecology and ecosystem carrying capacity as biology — this course computes a footprint balance against available productive land; the biology is HS Biology.
- Exponential-function notation for growth — this course uses the rule of 70 for doubling time; the algebra is Algebra 1, and Grade 8 Math withholds it by name.
- Statistical inference (significance, sampling) and the formal names of the aggregation fallacies (ecological fallacy, modifiable areal unit problem) — this course works scale-of-analysis arithmetic; the statistics are high-school / AP.
- GIS software procedures, spectral classification and remote-sensing algorithms — this course reasons over described layers, buffers, weighted site scores and land-cover change; the software is a high-school GIS elective.
- Any chronology: settlement eras, wars, presidencies, immigration eras, industrialization, treaties by name — this course reads present-day patterns and, where history explains a pattern, says so in one clause; the history is AP US History, World History and the legacy Grade 8 US-history seeds. This course is geography, not history.
- Civics (rights, responsibilities, participation in government) — touched nowhere here; Grade 7 World Geography and the legacy Grade 8 civics seed.
- Culture, language and religion at Grade 8 depth — deliberately not built; Grade 7 World Geography owns first exposure and AP Human Geography a deeper pass.
- Trade policy, tariffs, exchange rates, and any named trade agreement or live dispute, blockade or conflict — this course quantifies interdependence, chokepoints and dispute causes on described or long-settled cases only; policy and current events are out of the course entirely at any grade.
- Real statistics attached to real named places — every figure in an item is described in the item and belongs to an invented place, or is verified and sourced; the only real named patterns are North America's long-settled physical regions, drainage, hazard regions and settlement trends, stated in words.
