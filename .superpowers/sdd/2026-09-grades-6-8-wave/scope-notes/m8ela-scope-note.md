Exclusion shape: vertical along a cumulative strand. Each Grade 8 unit continues the same-numbered Grade 7 strand (assumed below, never re-taught) and stops short of the HS English row above it. Objective summary is not re-taught anywhere in this course.

**Strand 1 — Reading literature: evidence, character & structure**
- Below (G7, assumed): explicit-vs-inferred evidence and citing exact wording (RL.7.1); the five plot stages, turning-point test, and four conflict types (RL.7.3); the five indirect-characterization moves and the "words vs. actions" trap (RL.7.3); setting as a limit on and revealer of character (RL.7.3).
- Above (HS): inference combined with background knowledge (RI.9-10.1); motive inference and direct-vs-indirect characterization as an analytic frame (RL.9-10.3); flashback and foreshadowing as structural choices (RL.9-10.5); enjambment, end-stopped lines, sonnet and haiku (RL.9-10.5).
- Declined at G8: RL.8.7 (a filmed or live production vs. the text) — the course carries no media and the standard requires the student to have watched something.

**Strand 2 — Reading literature: theme, point of view & figurative language**
- Below (G7): stating a theme as a sentence, the moral-as-command trap, and the objective summary (RL.7.2); the four narration types by "whose thoughts" and contrasting two characters' points of view (RL.7.6); simile, metaphor, personification, hyperbole, idiom (RL.7.4); tone vs. mood with a precise adjective (RL.7.4).
- Above (HS): motifs and repeated images across a whole text (RL.9-10.2); the unreliable narrator (RL.9-10.6); sensory imagery and symbolism (RL.9-10.4); alliteration, assonance, rhyme, rhythm (RL.9-10.4); whole-work study of a source text behind a modern retelling (outside this catalog).

**Strand 3 — Reading informational texts: central idea, features & vocabulary**
- Below (G7): identifying a central idea as a full sentence (RI.7.2); the objective summary of nonfiction (RI.7.2); what each text feature and graphic contributes (RI.7.5/7.7); determining a technical word's meaning (RI.7.4).
- Above (HS): central idea at 9-10 register with inference (RI.9-10.2); "why this structure and not another" (RI.9-10.5/6); paragraph unity as a writing skill (W.9-10.2b); register matched to audience (L.9-10.3).

**Strand 4 — Reading informational texts: structure, purpose, argument & comparison**
- Below (G7): the five whole-text structures from signal words (RI.7.5); purpose and perspective by inclusions, omissions, and loaded words (RI.7.6); evidence relevance and sufficiency (RI.7.8); two accurate texts compared by emphasis and interpretation (RI.7.9).
- Above (HS): named logical fallacies — ad hominem, straw man, false dilemma, bandwagon, hasty generalization, slippery slope, false cause (RI.9-10.8); ethos, pathos, logos (RI.9-10.6/8); lateral reading and corroboration of the source itself (W.9-10.8/RI.9-10.8).
- Declined at G8: analysis of ACTUAL multimedia under RI.8.7 — the medium row reasons about media described in words only.

**Strand 5 — Grammar & usage**
- Below (G7): parts of speech by job (L.7.1a); subject-verb agreement through interrupters, compounds, indefinites, collectives, inversion (L.7.1); pronoun-antecedent agreement and ambiguous/missing reference repair (L.7.1); tense choice, tense consistency, irregular past and past participle (L.7.1/L.7.3a).
- Above (HS): perfect tenses and signal-word-licensed shifts (L.9-10.1); who/whom and pronoun case at HS register (L.9-10.1); misplaced and dangling modifiers, parallel form across infinitives and -ing forms (L.9-10.1b).

**Strand 6 — Sentence structure & punctuation**
- Below (G7): phrase vs. clause by the subject-verb test, independent vs. dependent by the starter word (L.7.1a); the four sentence types by clause count and combining with the joining word that carries the meaning (L.7.1b); fragments, run-ons, comma splices and the three legal joins (L.7.1c); the five comma jobs, end marks, apostrophes (L.7.2/L.7.2a). Also assumed from G6: a PAIR of commas, parentheses, or dashes to set off extra information (L.6.2a).
- Above (HS): matched pairs of dashes or parentheses never mixed, and quotation-mark punctuation (L.9-10.2b); semicolons with conjunctive adverbs and colons after a complete clause (L.9-10.2a); apostrophes and possessives at HS register (L.9-10.2c); precision and concision revision (L.9-10.3).
- Declined at G8: L.8.2c (spell correctly) — no MS row gives spelling a lesson; the spelling half of confusables lives in G7's commonly-confused-words row.

**Strand 7 — Vocabulary in context & word study**
- Below (G7): the four context-clue types and substitution check (L.7.4a); the four-step morphology routine, eight prefixes, nine roots (L.7.4b); polarity connotation — positive, neutral, negative (L.7.5c); its/it's, your/you're, there/their/they're, whose/who's, then/than, affect/effect (L.7.4). Also assumed from G6: word relationships and analogies (L.6.5b), so L.8.5b gets no row.
- Above (HS): the HS confusable set — accept/except, lose/loose, fewer/less, allusion/illusion (L.9-10.1/2); near-synonym choice for an intended effect at HS register (L.9-10.5b); register for a named audience (L.9-10.3).

**Strand 8 — Argument writing**
- Below (G7): the debatable one-sentence claim and two or three distinct reasons (W.7.1a); five evidence types plus the elaboration sentence (W.7.1b); the counterclaim/rebuttal two-move, the negative-restatement fake, and the two legal rebuttal moves by reasoning (W.7.1a); paragraph order, counterclaim placement, and the conclusion that says what follows (W.7.1c/e). W.8.1e (the concluding statement) therefore gets no G8 row; G6 and G7 own it.
- Above (HS): the arguable, specific, provable, previewing thesis (W.9-10.1a); rebuttal by exposing a hidden assumption (W.9-10.1b); claims-evidence-reasoning at HS register (W.9-10.1).

**Strand 9 — Informative & narrative writing**
- Below (G7): focus statement, narrowing, the five structures, paragraph-level grouping (W.7.2a); paragraph development and cutting the off-topic sentence (W.7.2b); the seven transition families and pronoun threads (W.7.2c); dialogue that does a job, pacing, sensory description, precise words (W.7.3b/d). W.8.2c (varied transitions) and W.8.2f (informative conclusion) therefore get no G8 row; W.8.2e (formal style) is served by the argument-writing formal-style row.
- Above (HS): hook-context-thesis openings and synthesis-significance-call-to-action closings (W.9-10.2a/f); topic-sentence unity (W.9-10.2b); cohesion by repeated key terms (W.9-10.2c); flashback and foreshadowing as analyzed structure (RL.9-10.5).

**Strand 10 — Research & citation**
- Below (G7): topic vs. question, narrowing, open not yes/no, answerable (W.7.7); the four credibility questions, primary vs. secondary, "credible for what" (W.7.8); quote/paraphrase/summarize, the look-away paraphrase, all three get credit (W.7.8); what needs a citation, common knowledge, the five pieces, credit in two places, note-taking hygiene (W.7.8). The plagiarism-avoidance half of W.8.8 is therefore G7's and gets no G8 row.
- Above (HS): matching a question to primary/secondary and scholarly/popular sources (W.9-10.7/8); lateral reading (W.9-10.8); patchwriting as plagiarism (W.9-10.8); the frame-quote-explain structure and the common-knowledge test (W.9-10.8).

**Across all strands**
- Speaking & Listening (SL.8, all standards) — not covered, consistent with the shipped Grade 6 and Grade 7 courses, neither of which has an SL unit.
- W.8.4-W.8.6 (production, revision with guidance, technology) and W.8.10 (range of writing) — process standards with no discrete lesson-sized objective; not covered, as in G6 and G7.
- L.8.6 (general academic and domain-specific vocabulary acquisition) — a cross-course habit, not a lesson; served incidentally by the informational word-choice, dictionary-verification, and precise-language rows.
