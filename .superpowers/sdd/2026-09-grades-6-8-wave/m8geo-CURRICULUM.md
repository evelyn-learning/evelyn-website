# Grade 8 World Geography — Curriculum Table (Grades 6/8 wave, Grade 8 drop)

## ⚠️ SCOPING DECISION FOR SIGN-OFF

**The conflict.** The signed Grade 6 table (`m6geo-CURRICULUM.md`, "Grade 8 implication") left Grade 8 two candidate seats and explicitly did not rule between them: (1) the one world region Grade 7's sweep never claims — North America / the United States & Canada — built in parallel to G7 Units 7–10; or (2) an applied, issues-based course (GIS, sustainability, geopolitics, planning at scale) extending G6 Unit 7 (Technology & Data Skills) and Unit 10 (Applying Geography). Grade 7 (`m7geo-*.ts`, 40 seeds, all file names read and all 40 LO descriptions read) is a complete first-exposure world survey: Units 1–6 thematic, Units 7–10 regional. There is no unclaimed *topic* left at first-exposure depth; every G7 seed opens from zero, as the G6 table found and as the two G7 seeds read in full here confirm again (`m7geo-u3-population-distribution-and-density.ts` invents every number and teaches THAT an average hides clustering; `m7geo-u7-latin-america-economy-and-cities.ts` teaches export concentration as a risk-not-rank using two invented countries). So Grade 8 cannot be a third first-exposure pass; it has to be either a new region, a new depth, or both.

**Options weighed** (the two seats the G6 table left, plus the blend the controller recommended, each tested against the files):

- **A. Pure North America regional course (10 units on the US & Canada).** Rejected. G7 gives every world region exactly four rows (physical geography, history & culture, economy & cities, environment/issues — see the 7.1–10.4 rows of `GEO-FANOUT-CONTRACT.md`). Stretching one region to 40 rows at geography depth forces the course into either trivia (state-by-state facts) or, far more likely, into chronology — and the codebase already ships United States history at exactly this grade and above: the legacy `g8-ss-*` seeds are ALL US history and civics (`g8-ss-westward-expansion.ts`, `g8-ss-immigration-industrial.ts`, `g8-ss-civil-war.ts`, `g8-ss-civics-rights-responsibilities.ts`, etc., `topic: 'us-history'` / `'civics'`, imported in `store.ts`), and `ap-apush-*` (AP US History) sits above them. A 40-row North America course would drift into that lane by week three. It would also collide from below with `g4-ss-us-regions.ts` (Grade 4, `topic: 'us-geography'`, "Identify regions of the United States by location, climate, and resources") unless every row stays well above identify depth.
- **B. Pure applied/issues course (no North America).** Viable, but it leaves the only unclaimed region on Earth unclaimed for a third time, and it would make the shipped G7 sweep the *only* regional geography a student ever gets — with their own continent missing. The G6 table flagged North America as open ground twice; declining it needs a reason, and there is none.
- **C. Blended course whose spine is vertical BY DEPTH, plus North America at G7's regional depth (the controller's recommendation).** Tested against the files and **adopted**, with one adjustment described below. The depth ladder is real and readable in the seeds: G6 = define / identify / classify / locate (the G6 contract's five tests); G7 = the mechanism, taught from zero (five climate controls, three boundary types, push/pull, density = population ÷ area, indicators and composites, natural/geometric/cultural boundaries); G8 = **analysis, evaluation and application on described data and cases**. Concretely: where G7 3.1 says an average *can* hide clustering, G8 1.3 hands the student the sub-regional figures and asks which scale supports which claim. Where G7 6.3 lists the four predictable causes of a boundary dispute, G8 9.1 gives a described case and asks which cause the evidence supports and which resolution mechanism fits. Where G7 5.4 traces a supply chain, G8 8.1 quantifies why two described places both gain from specialization and evaluates the chain's single point of failure. This is the same relationship G7 U7–10 have to G6 U9 (locate → content), one rung up (mechanism → analysis), and it is the only shape in which "G8 revisits population" is not a defect.

**The adjustment: TWO North America units, not three.** The controller's plausible shape was 2–3 North America units plus 7–8 applied units. I chose 2 (Units 3–4: physical systems; people, cities & economy) and put North America's *environmental issues* — the row that would have been the G7-parallel "Environment & Change" row — into the global units as cases instead (river-basin allocation in 6.1 is structured on a Colorado-type basin; wildfire and hurricane exposure in 7.1 and 3.4; sea-level in 7.3). Reason: at G8 depth a North America environment row and a global sustainability row on the same theme would be the same lesson with a different label, which is exactly the vertical-by-depth defect this table exists to avoid horizontally. The cost is that North America gets 8 rows where G7 gives each region 4 — more coverage, not a different kind of coverage, and justified by the audience's home region — and that no row is *titled* North America environment. The owner may prefer a third NA unit; the swap would be to fold Unit 10 (capstone) into Units 1–2 and 7 and give the freed unit to "North America: Environment & Change" (water in the West, the Great Lakes, the Arctic and permafrost, wildfire). Flagged in For sign-off #1.

**What this scope refuses, and why.** (i) It is not history: no row is a chronology, no row names a war, a presidency, a treaty by name, or a settlement-era narrative; history enters only as G7's contract allows, "as the reason a place looks the way it does today," and the North America rows are physical regions, climate, watersheds, hazards, population distribution, migration flows, metropolitan form and economic regions. (ii) It does not reach into AP Human Geography — a finding beyond the brief: the seed library carries an `ap-human-geography` course (`ap-human-geo-population.ts`, `-urban.ts`, `-agriculture.ts`, `-political.ts`; `grade: '11'`, imported in `store.ts`) whose LO descriptions own the demographic transition model, urban-structure models, agricultural land-use models and the state/nation/nation-state distinction. Those named models are withheld by name in the Scope cells and listed under Explicitly excluded. (iii) It never needs a map or an image: every row is written so its items reason over a described table, a described choropleth, a described transect or a described scenario, which also keeps the independent fact-verification burden on *reasoning* rather than on memorised world facts (the contract's rule 1 and rule 3 — every real-place claim true, no invented statistics about real places — still bind every plan).

## Progression rationale (deepens Grade 7; feeds high school)

- **Data & spatial analysis (Unit 1) → deepens G7 U1 and the "average hides" idea in G7 3.1/5.3**: G7 taught the tools and stated the caveats; G8 evaluates the tool's choices (classing, projection, denominator, scale) against a claim. Feeds AP Statistics-style reasoning and AP Human Geography's data-interpretation questions.
- **GIS & geospatial reasoning (Unit 2)**: the seat G6 U7 reserved for Grade 8 and G7 never touched. Layers, buffers, weighted site selection and change detection, all as reasoning on described data. Feeds any high-school GIS elective; the software itself is left there.
- **North America (Units 3–4) → parallels G7 U7–10 one rung up**: physical regions explained by plate position and climate controls (G7 U2 mechanisms applied), watersheds and hazards mapped to regions, population and migration read from data with G7 U3's tools, metropolitan form measured, economic regions classified with G7 U5's typology. Feeds AP Human Geography's North American cases and stays clear of `ap-apush-*` and the legacy `g8-ss-*` history.
- **Population & migration at scale (Unit 5) → deepens G7 U3**: pyramids quantified, projections evaluated, flows and displacement analyzed. Feeds `ap-human-geo-population.ts` (demographic transition model).
- **Resources, energy & sustainability (Unit 6) → deepens G7 5.2 and 7.4**: allocation arithmetic, energy trade-offs, footprint balance, food systems. Feeds `ap-human-geo-agriculture.ts` and `bio-u9-*` ecology.
- **Climate risk & resilience (Unit 7) → deepens G7 2.2 and 7.4**: risk decomposed, trends read, exposure counted, responses evaluated. Feeds high-school Earth/environmental science (mechanism) and civics (policy).
- **Globalization & development (Unit 8) → deepens G7 U5 and 9.4/10.3**: specialization quantified, composites constructed, siting decided, connectivity measured. Feeds `ap-human-geo-*` economic and industrial units.
- **Geopolitics (Unit 9) → deepens G7 U6 and the location ideas in 5.4/7.3/8.4**: dispute cases, maritime geometry, cooperation design, chokepoint dependence. Feeds `ap-human-geo-political.ts`.
- **Capstone (Unit 10) → deepens G6 U10**: argument, plan evaluation, regional comparison, scenario analysis — the course's terminal synthesis.

## Explicitly excluded (owned above, or by a sibling course)

The exclusion shape for this subject is **vertical by depth**: each item below is a theme this course *does* touch, listed with the depth or the named model this course stops short of and the file that owns it.

- The stage-numbered **demographic transition model** — this course reads pyramids and dependency (5.1) and evaluates projections (5.2); the model is AP Human Geography `ap-human-geo-population.ts`.
- **Urban-structure models** (concentric zone, sector, multiple nuclei) and gentrification — this course measures urban form and evaluates a growth proposal (4.3, 10.2); the models are `ap-human-geo-urban.ts`.
- **Agricultural land-use models** and the formal agricultural-systems typology — this course compares two described systems on data (6.4); the models are `ap-human-geo-agriculture.ts`.
- **State / nation / nation-state** and supranational-organization analysis by name — this course analyzes dispute cases and cooperation design (9.1, 9.3); the typology is `ap-human-geo-political.ts`.
- **Industrial-location theory by name** — this course decides a described siting on factors (8.3); the theory is high-school / AP.
- **The greenhouse mechanism and attribution** — this course reads climate trend data (7.2); no MS science course currently owns the mechanism (`m6sci` 10.1-10.2 give the carbon cycle and the evidence only; `m8sci` excludes climate by design — see For sign-off #7).
- **Hazard physics and forecasting** (how a hurricane, tornado or earthquake happens; weather-map forecasting) — this course maps hazard regions and evaluates risk (3.4, U7); the physics is `m6sci` U6 and U9 (`how-air-mass-interactions-produce-severe-weather`, `forecasting-and-preparing-for-weather-hazards`) and plate mechanics G7 2.2.
