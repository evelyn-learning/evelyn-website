Answer: 1, 3, 5, 8 — ΔH is positive, the reaction is endothermic, Ea is greater than ΔH, and the products have more potential energy than the reactants. Recorded as 1358.

  1. ▼

    1. Read the diagram

    The vertical axis is potential energy (the chemical energy stored in the bonds of the substances, Ep, in kilojoules); the horizontal axis is reaction progress. The curve starts at the reactants' level, rises over a hill and settles at the products' level.

    The nine descriptions come in groups that exclude one another: 1 or 2 (sign of ΔH), 3 or 4 (type of reaction), 5, 6 or 7 (Ea against ΔH), 8 or 9 (which level is higher). One from each group fits the diagram.

  2. ▼

    2. Compare the two levels (description 8)

    Extend each flat level across the diagram. The products' level is above the reactants' level, so Ep of the products is greater than Ep of the reactants. Description 8.

  3. ▼

    3. The enthalpy change (descriptions 1 and 3)

    ΔH = Ep(products) − Ep(reactants)
    Products minus reactants, always in that order. The arrow is drawn from the reactants' level to the products' level.
    ΔH > 0
    The products' level is higher, so the subtraction gives a positive number: the arrow points up.

    Tap an equation line to see the operation behind it.

    A positive enthalpy change (ΔH, the energy absorbed or released by the reaction at constant pressure) means the system absorbed energy from its surroundings. That is an endothermic (energy flows in; the products store more energy than the reactants did) reaction. Descriptions 1 and 3.

    Why is the sign read from the direction of the arrow?

    Because ΔH is a difference, products minus reactants. An arrow drawn from the reactants' level to the products' level points in the direction of that difference: up for positive, down for negative. Reading the sign from the picture is the same as doing the subtraction.

  4. ▼

    4. The activation energy (description 5)

    The activation energy (Ea, the least energy the reactants need to reach the activated complex at the top of the hill) is measured from the reactants' level to the peak. On this diagram that climb is taller than the rise to the products, so Ea > ΔH. Description 5.

    Could Ea ever be smaller than ΔH here?

    Not for an endothermic reaction. The path has to pass through the peak before it can reach the products' level, so the peak is at least as high as the products. Ea is therefore at least ΔH; it equals ΔH only if there is no hump above the products at all.

    Check yourself first

    If the products' level were drawn below the reactants' level instead, which description would replace 1?

  5. ▼

    5. Record the answer

    The descriptions that fit are 1, 3, 5 and 8. In increasing numerical order: 1358.

Sanity check

Energy on the vertical axis goes up the page. Products above reactants means energy went in, and only an endothermic reaction can do that, so 1 and 3 must go together and 8 agrees with them. The peak is well above the products, so 5 is consistent with the picture too.

One common slip

Measuring the activation energy from the products instead of the reactants. Ea is always the climb from the reactants' level to the peak. The drop from the peak to the products is the activation energy of the reverse reaction, a different quantity.

Try one more

An energy diagram shows the reactants at 50 kJ, the peak at 120 kJ and the products at 20 kJ. Give ΔH and Ea, and name the type of reaction.

Show the worked answer

ΔH = 20 − 50 = −30 kJ, so the reaction is exothermic. Ea = 120 − 50 = 70 kJ, measured from the reactants' level to the peak. Ea (70 kJ) is greater than ΔH (−30 kJ), as it always is when ΔH is negative. Whether Ea is also larger than the size of ΔH depends on the diagram; here it is.

The idea to remember

Read an energy diagram from the reactants' level: up to the peak is Ea; up or down to the products' level is ΔH, and its direction gives the sign and the type of reaction.

Diploma note: a "select the descriptions" numerical-response item is a set of exclusive groups. Decide each group from the diagram (sign, type, Ea against ΔH, which level is higher), then record the chosen digits in increasing order.
